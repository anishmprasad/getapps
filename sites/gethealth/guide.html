How to explore the body with GetHealth

A free 3D anatomy atlas of the human body, the frog and the cockroach — with a private lab report scanner that shows which organs your test results relate to.

Open the human atlasScan a lab report

What's inside

Every structure in GetHealth is a separate 3D part with its own description, what it does, a fact for younger learners and — for muscles — its origin, insertion, action and nerve supply. Paired structures come as a left and a right part.

Getting around

  • Rotate by dragging, zoom with the scroll wheel or a pinch, and pan with a right-drag or two fingers.
  • Click any part to open its card. Isolate structure hides everything else so you can turn it over on its own; Hide removes just that part.
  • Switch whole systems on and off in the left panel, or jump to a preset such as Skeleton or Organs. Hover a system and press Only to see it alone.
  • Drag the Explode anatomy slider to pull the body apart until every part is laid out in a grid — the quickest way to see how many pieces make up a skeleton.
  • X-ray makes everything see-through except your selection. Labels names the main structures on screen.
  • Search finds any structure by name, including everyday names such as “windpipe”, “womb” or “Adam's apple”.
  • Every structure has its own link. Copy it from the card to send someone straight to, say, the left femur.

Keyboard: / search · R reset view · X x-ray · L labels · F focus · I isolate · H hide · Esc close.

For students

The frog and cockroach models follow the animals studied in school biology — Rana tigrina and Periplaneta americana — including the structures examiners love: the frog's three-chambered heart, urostyle and fat bodies, and the cockroach's 13-chambered heart, Malpighian tubules, gizzard, spiracles, mushroom gland and ootheca. Use the sex switch to compare male and female reproductive systems, and the quiz to test yourself.

For kids

Turn on Kids mode in the systems panel. Cards lead with a simple fact, labels switch on, and the quiz asks for whole organs — “Find the heart!” — rather than individual chambers.

The lab report scanner

Open the report scanner and drop in a PDF, a photo or a screenshot of a blood or urine report, or paste its text. GetHealth reads it inside your browser — typed PDFs with pdf.js, photos and scans with on-device text recognition — and matches each line against its library of tests.

  • Values are converted to one unit system automatically (for example platelets reported in lakhs, glucose in mmol/L or creatinine in µmol/L).
  • If your report prints its own reference range, that range is used. For tests with clinical decision bands — HbA1c, fasting glucose, cholesterol, vitamin D, eGFR — the standard bands are used instead.
  • Results are grouped into high, low and in range, each with what the test measures and what an unusual result can mean. Switch on Show on body to light the related organs up in 3D: red for high, blue for low, green for in range.
  • Password-protected PDFs need unlocking first — GetPDF does that privately too.

GetHealth is an educational tool, not a medical device. It cannot diagnose anything. Reference ranges vary between labs, ages and circumstances, and a result slightly outside a range is often normal for that person. Always discuss your results with a doctor.

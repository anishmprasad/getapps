Privacy & data

The short version: your lab reports are read inside your browser and never uploaded, so there is nothing for us to keep, leak or hand over.

Your lab reports

GetHealth has no upload endpoint. When you add a report, the file is read into your tab's memory: PDFs with pdf.js, photos and scanned pages with Tesseract text recognition running on your device. The recognised text is matched against a test library that your browser has already downloaded.

  • Your report, its text and your results are never transmitted to us or to anyone else.
  • Nothing about your report is written to local storage, cookies or a cache. Closing or reloading the tab wipes it.
  • Your browser does download code and data to do this: the 3D engine, pdf.js and — only if you add a photo or scan — the text-recognition engine and its English language data, from public content delivery networks (jsDelivr and the Tesseract project). Those requests contain no report data.
  • You can check this yourself: open your browser's developer tools, watch the network panel and scan a report.

What we do collect

  • Two preferences in your browser's local storage — your colour theme and whether Kids mode is on. They never leave your device.
  • Aggregate analytics through Google Analytics: page views, approximate country, device type and referrer. No report contents and no results ever reach a network request. Google's policies govern that data, and an ad blocker or browser opt-out will stop it.

Not medical advice

GetHealth is an educational resource. It explains what tests measure and which parts of the body they relate to; it does not diagnose, treat or predict any condition, and it is not a substitute for a qualified clinician. Reference ranges differ between laboratories, and a result outside a range is not necessarily abnormal for you.

Children

The anatomy atlas, Kids mode and quiz are suitable for children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age.

Open source components

GetHealth uses three.js (MIT licence), pdf.js (Apache License 2.0) and tesseract.js (Apache License 2.0). We are grateful for them and not affiliated with those projects.
